Digital and Self-Archiving Policy

We recognize the importance of open access to scholarly research and the dissemination of knowledge. Our digital and self-archiving policy is designed to facilitate the sharing of published content while maintaining the integrity of the journal's publication process. This policy outlines the terms under which authors, readers, and institutions can archive and share our published articles.

Preprints and Postprints: Authors are encouraged to share preprints of their manuscripts on preprint servers before or during the peer review process. After publication, authors are allowed to share the final accepted manuscript (postprint) on personal websites, institutional repositories, and noncommercial subject repositories.

Accepted Manuscripts: Authors are permitted to deposit the accepted version of their manuscript in institutional or subject repositories immediately upon acceptance. This version must include all changes made during peer review but may not incorporate the final publisher's formatting and layout.

The copyright form can be obtained from the Copyright Form link.

Once completed and signed, the copyright form should be submitted online during the manuscript submission process.

Embargoes and Access Control: While authors are encouraged to share their manuscripts, the journal may impose an embargo period before the accepted version can be made publicly available. During this embargo period, access may be restricted to preserve the value of subscription-based content.

Attribution and Linking: When self-archiving, authors are required to provide proper attribution to the published article and include a link to the version of record on the journal's website. This ensures that readers can easily access the final, formatted version

Commercial Use and Licensing: Authors retain copyright to their work and grant the journal a license to publish. However, self-archiving does not affect an author's right to use their work for non-commercial purposes or for educational and scholarly activities.

Preservation and Archiving: Authors and institutions are encouraged to preserve the published article in reliable digital archives or repositories. This helps ensure long-term access to the research and supports the scholarly record.

Exceptions and Restrictions: In some cases, certain articles (e.g., those with third-party content or specific licensing agreements) may be subject to restrictions on self-archiving. Authors are advised to consult with the journal's editorial office regarding any potential limitations.

Digital Preservation Efforts: We are committed to preserving the digital versions of its published content. We collaborate with recognized digital preservaion initiatives to ensure the long-term availability and accessibility of our articles.

Compliance with Policies: Authors, readers, and institutions are responsible for complying with the terms and conditions outlined in this policy and any relevant copyright and licensing agreements.

Continuous Review: This digital and self-archiving policy will be periodically reviewed and updated to align with evolving best practices and technological advancements in the field of scholarly publishing.

By adhering to this policy, [Your Journal Name] aims to support the widespread dissemination of knowledge, promote open access, and contribute to the advancement of scholarly communication while respecting copyright and intellectual property rights.
